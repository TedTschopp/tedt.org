PRINCIPLE 01 / 13
MAKE EVERYONE ELSE SMARTER
01
If every decision needs you, you built a dependency
The architect’s value is capability multiplied.
EXPERT MODEL
I know.
I decide.
I approve.
AMPLIFIER MODEL
We understand.
We compare.
We decide.
DISCUSS
01 Where are we increasing the team’s judgment?
02 Where have we quietly become the bottleneck?

PRINCIPLE 02 / 13
LOWER RISK, DON’T MONOPOLIZE DECISIONS
02
Risk visibility beats risk ownership
Lower risk without taking away agency.
GATEKEEPER
The architect decides so nothing goes wrong.
GUIDE
The team sees the risk and chooses deliberately.
DISCUSS
01 Which risks should the team be equipped to own?
02 Where are controls unintentionally removing agency?

PRINCIPLE 03 / 13
FRAME THE SOLUTION SPACE BEFORE SELECTING
03
Widen the map before choosing the road
Frame viable options and decision dimensions before narrowing.
PREMATURE ANSWER
Start with a preferred product and prove it right.
FRAMED SPACE
Start with requirements, constraints, options, and trade-offs.
DISCUSS
01 Which dimensions actually shape this decision?
02 Which viable option do we tend to exclude too early?

05
PRINCIPLE 04 / 13
DISTINGUISH INHERENT FROM ACCIDENTAL COMPLEXITY
04
Complexity has two species—and only one deserves mercy
Make inherent complexity intuitive. Remove accidental complexity.
INHERENT
Belongs to the domain.
Name it. Model it. Make it intuitive.
ACCIDENTAL
Added by us.
Expose it. Challenge it. Remove it.
DISCUSS
01 What complexity belongs to the problem itself?
02 What complexity did our organization or design add?

PRINCIPLE 05 / 13
VISUAL MODELS ACCELERATE SHARED UNDERSTANDING
05
A diagram is a test, not a decoration
Explicit semantics turn a picture into shared reasoning.
DECORATION
Boxes and arrows that look right but mean little.
MODEL
Boundaries, relationships, and flows with explicit meaning.
DISCUSS
01 What does every box, boundary, and arrow mean?
02 Which assumption is still hiding in prose?

PRINCIPLE 06 / 13
SCOUT WHEN CHANGE IS FAST
06
When maps age quickly, send scouts
Purpose-driven exploration beats a stale catalog.
CATALOG
Records what existed and what once appeared to fit.
SCOUT
Answers a live question in territory that is moving.
DISCUSS
01 Where has our catalog become stale or incomplete?
02 What specific question should the next scout answer?

PRINCIPLE 07 / 13
VALIDATE HEURISTICS CONTINUOUSLY
07
Yesterday’s shortcut can become today’s hidden risk
Heuristics are compressed experience. Give them an expiration date.
THEN
A trade-off was tested under known constraints.
NOW
The technology, cost, requirement, or constraint may have moved.
DISCUSS
01 Which trade-off is still living on old evidence?
02 What test would revalidate or retire that assumption?

PRINCIPLE 08 / 13
SUITABLE OR UNSUITABLE, NOT GOOD OR BAD
08
Architecture is fit, not fashion
Judge a design against the situation it must survive.
FASHION
Is it modern, elegant, popular, or theoretically perfect?
FIT
Does it meet the actual requirements, constraints, and trade-offs?
DISCUSS
01 Which requirement or constraint makes this suitable?
02 Are we judging the design—or our preference for it?

PRINCIPLE 09 / 13
DECISION QUALITY MATTERS MORE THAN CERTAINTY
09
Decision quality beats performative certainty
Transparent reasoning outlasts confident hand-waving.
CERTAINTY THEATER
One answer, no alternatives, and no visible conditions.
DECISION QUALITY
Reasoning, options, trade-offs, evidence, and revisit triggers.
DISCUSS
01 Could another team reconstruct why we chose this?
02 What new evidence should cause us to revisit it?

PRINCIPLE 10 / 13
EARN POLITICAL CAPITAL BEFORE SPENDING IT
10
Credibility is an architecture dependency
Challenge selectively. Deliver consistently. Build trust first.
EARN
Relevant help, sound judgment, consistent delivery, restraint.
SPEND
Push back when consequences are high and the evidence matters.
DISCUSS
01 Which decision is important enough to challenge now?
02 What useful delivery would earn trust before that moment?

PRINCIPLE 11 / 13
ARCHITECTS NEED CURRENT TECHNICAL SKILLS
11
Architecture without current practice becomes folklore
Hands-on knowledge tests remembered constraints against reality.
MEMORY
Guidance based on how platforms and trade-offs used to behave.
CURRENT PRACTICE
Small experiments that reveal how they behave now.
DISCUSS
01 Which constraint have we personally tested recently?
02 Where are we giving guidance from memory alone?

PRINCIPLE 12 / 13
CONSCIOUS SIMPLICITY IS STRENGTH
12
Simple may be evidence the hard work happened
Simplicity succeeds when complexity is resolved—not hidden.
HIDDEN
The diagram is simple because the difficult parts were ignored.
RESOLVED
The design is simple because unnecessary complexity was removed.
DISCUSS
01 Did we eliminate complexity or merely move it elsewhere?
02 Can the team explain why the simple design is sufficient?

PRINCIPLE 13 / 13
SOURCE CLAIMS ARE NOT APPROVAL EVIDENCE
13
A source can start a conversation. It cannot approve the design.
External examples motivate. Internal evidence authorizes.
SOURCE CLAIM
A talk, article, vendor example, or reference architecture.
APPROVAL EVIDENCE
Accountable review against internal requirements and thresholds.
DISCUSS
01 What internal evidence is required before approval?
02 Who is accountable for accepting the operating thresholds?
