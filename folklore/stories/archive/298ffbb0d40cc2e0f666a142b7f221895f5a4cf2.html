For a long time, the worried man had pondered back and forth how he might build a new house, however modest, but all his thought and deliberation led nowhere; he always lacked the necessary money. (In versions from the Isental and Reuss valley, it is a Gädemli.)", "One day, he had sat down in a field or in the forest—I do not know which is correct—tired from his hard work, and was brooding over how to solve this pressing problem. When he looked up, a strange little man was suddenly standing before him. As though he had read the troubled farmer's gloomy thoughts in his face, the stranger sympathetically asked the cause of his distress. The farmer honestly told him of his hardship and predicament. ‘Oh, pah,’ said the stranger, ‘that is easily remedied. Give me whatever you find behind the stove when you arrive home from work, and by the first cockcrow tomorrow, your new little house shall be standing there finished and ready. Shake on it—you are making a good bargain!’ After some thought, the farmer accepted the offered bargain and promised the peculiar helper what he demanded in return for the promised construction of a house. He was startled for a moment when, as they parted, he caught a fleeting glimpse of the two horse hooves—or goat hooves—of this kindhearted, remarkably skillful, and speedy carpenter. Yet he easily consoled himself, because nothing but a few rags, torn stockings, and old trousers was ever to be found behind his stove. The farmer soon left his work and returned to the hut he expected to inhabit for the last time that day. But how the poor man blanched when, behind the stove in the little room, he found a splendid newborn boy lying softly in a basket! Beside himself with grief and remorse, he lamented his nameless misery to his mother-in-law and confessed his rash, senseless bargain with the Evil One. The cunning woman comforted him and declared, ‘Just leave it to me; I am more than a match for that stupid devil.’ She made the vigilant house dog and the cock that heralded morning go without food. The pounding of the invisible devil's helpers, hard at work on the new house, kept the hungry dog from sleeping, and it barked at the top of its lungs. Scarcely had midnight properly struck when the cock too awoke amid all the noise and barking and crowed with all its might. It was high time: the new building already stood completed up to the roof, and fortunately for the little farmer, only a small hole at the ridge remained uncovered—just three shingles were missing. The Horned One scratched his head and said ruefully, ‘If I had done as much work with my own hands as I accomplished merely by watching, the little house would have been completed, and the child's soul would not have escaped me!’ (Or: ‘If I had known that, I would have hurried all the harder!’) The hole in the roof ridge could never be covered; it always had to remain open.", "Pastor Arnold", "b) According to another version, at cockcrow, the devil was just bringing the last doorpost, but he did not manage to set it properly on the ground.
