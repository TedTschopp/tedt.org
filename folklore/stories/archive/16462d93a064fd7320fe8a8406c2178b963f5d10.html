When he grew old and weak and weary of ruling, he summoned his three sons and told them that he would hand over the kingdom to whichever one went out and, within three days, made the finest and best carriage. Of the three sons, however, the two eldest were considered highly gifted, while no one thought very much of the youngest.", "The three now went outside the city, where the two elder brothers let the youngest go on alone. He entered the nearby forest and went ever deeper into the thicket, where he sank down exhausted beneath a tree and then brought out bread and cheese to refresh himself. An old, gray-haired man in a broad cloak came walking along and asked the king’s son for a bite to eat. The young man gladly gave the stranger what he had and watched with pleasure as the old man enjoyed the coarse fare. When the old man had eaten his fill, he said to the king’s son: \"I know your need, and I will help you. In the meantime, lie down and sleep until I wake you.\" The young man slept for a long time, and when he awoke, the old man had disappeared; before him, however, stood the most comfortable and magnificent carriage imaginable. He now drew it into the city and brought it before the king. Before the king’s throne stood the other two brothers, who had scarcely managed to make a wheel, let alone an entire carriage. The father praised the supposed fool very highly and wished to award him the kingdom; but the other two sons begged and pleaded until the old king permitted a second trial. He handed each son a bundle of hemp, announcing that the kingdom would go to whichever one produced the finest spun thread from it. Once again the three brothers set out, this time each going his own way. The youngest went back to the forest, but he was deeply troubled and sat down beside a pond to weep. A little frog hopped out of the water and asked the cause of his sorrow. When the frog had heard the prince’s tale of woe, it told him to take heart and boldly throw the bundle of hemp into the pond. The king’s son did so, whereupon sleep overcame him. After many, many hours he awoke, rubbed his eyes, and was greatly astonished to see the most marvelous golden thread imaginable. The king’s son took it and went to his father’s house. His brothers had also brought handsome thread, but it could not compare at all with the youngest son’s golden thread. Once again the father wished to award him the prize, and once again he yielded to the elder brothers’ pleas and allowed a third and final trial. The old king swore by his beard that whichever of his three sons brought him the most beautiful daughter-in-law would inherit the kingdom. With this decision, the princes went out into the countryside at the same time through three different city gates. The youngest did not deliberate long but strode briskly toward the forest and the pond. When he arrived there, he called for the little frog, which came at once and asked what he desired. The prince explained his concern, whereupon the frog said: \"Kiss me on the mouth and trust me.\" The young man did as he was told, and before he knew it, the little frog had changed into the loveliest and most beautiful maiden. He went to court with her, and because his bride far surpassed his brothers’ brides in beauty, he received his father’s kingdom and lived splendidly and joyfully with his wife." ], "notes": [] } } ---
