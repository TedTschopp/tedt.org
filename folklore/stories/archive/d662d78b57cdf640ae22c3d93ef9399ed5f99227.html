To arrive early enough, they hurried on those days to get up and perform the necessary chores, then set out at once on the path to church. They usually reached the house of God too early rather than too late, entered devoutly, and remained in prayer until the service began. Then one day a wealthy foreign gentleman appeared on this Alp with a guide and stayed there for several days. When he took his leave, he showed himself highly satisfied and promised to return in a year with a gift. And when the gentleman arrived the next summer—what had he brought the good people? It was a little bell with a bright, clear tone. What joy there was when it rang the Ave for the first time! They entertained their benefactor in the friendliest fashion with sweet Alpine fare. Nothing was spared: geblähte Nidel, Kohlermuss, Burehögerli, Stunggäwerni, Kniesalb, Fusterli, Fusterlikossi, and Zänzänä—gentian brandy—were all served. After several days, the noble gentleman departed once more, and the Älpler charged an old, pious Senn with giving him the Ehrenweg. The old man pulled a sour face at this and showed no great enthusiasm for his assignment, but he carried it out. As they parted, he said to the gentleman: “I know you. You are the Evil Enemy; denial will not help you. But tell me why you gave us the bell. I adjure you!” And the Evil One had to confess the truth: from then on, the people would rely upon the bell’s summons and consequently would no longer come so early to church—indeed, they would often come too late. Many a prayer would go unsaid.
