They then sit together in long rows in their bathing shirts, some immersed to the neck, others to the pit of the stomach. Stone benches run in a square along the walls; strangers and visitors stroll outside along the passageways; and a bathmaster supervises his patients, whose customs are so varied. But it is not only the poor who resort to this one spring among the many like it in the city of Baden; young wives who long for an heir also try secretly to gain admission here. During the night, when the bath attendants let the used water drain away and have rinsed clean the floor and stone seats, the young woman comes with her maid while all inquisitive onlookers are asleep and presses a coin into the attendant’s hand. He understands the hint. Once she is dressed in her bathing attire, a long shirt of fine wool, he leads her to the Verenaloch, where the hot spring rises from the ground. She lowers one leg into the pipe and lets it become thoroughly warm; she then confidently hopes that this act will help bring about the swift fulfillment of her maternal wishes.
