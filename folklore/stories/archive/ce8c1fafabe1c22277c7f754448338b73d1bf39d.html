The holding formerly belonged to Michel von Ried, who was a prosperous farmer. Every winter Michel fed out the first hay on this remote mountain property. Once, when the Waldbruder was staying with him, his neighbor Nazhans tended the cattle for him. He remained overnight in the narrow little parlor with its three small windows. Not everyone would have dared spend the night utterly alone on that lonely mountain holding. But Nazhans was a large, strong man who could fear neither the living nor the dead. Finally he gave holy water to the arme Seelen and, in a loud, strong voice, wished them eternal rest. Without fear he put out the light and lay down to sleep.", "During the night Nazhans awoke. Had there not been a knock on the eastern wall? Before the herdsman could think anything good or bad, the house door and the parlor door had already opened. As if to ward something off, he extended his right arm. Then it seemed to him that another ice-cold hand pressed upon his own right hand, then another, and another—more than twenty, more than a hundred hands—while he could neither defend himself nor distinguish anyone in the darkness. When the doors finally closed again and Nazhans could draw his arm back beneath the bedcover, his own hand had become cold as a glacier. The next morning he searched in vain for the nocturnal visitors’ tracks in the freshly fallen snow. Some years later, Nazhans was once walking through the broad gully of the Mühlebach in the Mittliwald. It was Hornung, when the days are already growing longer and the sun’s rays soften the hard snow. As he reached the middle of the slope descending from Fluhmatten toward the Mühlebach, he heard a Chlapf—a bang—above him and, to his horror, saw high up on the slope a break as broad and tall as a man in the white cover of snow. A Gwächte had just broken loose. Hissing, it now rushed toward him, swept both legs from beneath him, and hurled him over cliffs and slopes, thundering and crashing, carrying stones and tree trunks along in furious bounds toward the valley floor. Again and again heavy masses of snow rolled over the defenseless man, who could extend only his right hand. Each time he threatened to suffocate, a cold hand pulled him upward and set him back atop the rolling avalanche. Only in the Binden above Wiler did the masses of snow spread out and come to rest.
