In a mine in the Graubünden Alps, he often appeared and was particularly busy on Fridays pouring the excavated ore from one bucket into another. The owner of the mine was not permitted to take offense at this but was also never harmed by him. Once, however, when a worker, angered by this futile activity, scolded and cursed the spirit, it seized him with such force that although he did not die, his face was turned backward. In the Annaberg, in the gallery called the Rosary, he breathed upon twelve miners while they worked, whereupon they fell dead, and although the mine was rich in silver, it was not worked any further. Here, he showed himself in the form of a long-necked horse with dreadful eyes staring from its forehead. At Schneeberg, however, he appeared as a black monk in the St. George Mine and seized a miner, lifted him from the ground, and set him down so hard in the upper part of the mine—which in former times had been very rich in silver—that his limbs were injured. In the Harz, he once punished a wicked Steiger who tormented the miners.
