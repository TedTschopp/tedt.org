They are called the Bettwil and Schongau Baths after the nearby settlement and because their founders are said to have come from Schongau; the Weissenbach Baths after the home of the villain related here; and the Guggi Baths either because of the breadth of the view through gaps in the fir forest on one side, after the Gugger, meaning Satan, or because the Bluebeard called Rüefengügges lived here, whose face was fiery red from rage and leprosy. In former times, they were called the Pagan Baths and the Fountain of the Eleven Thousand Maidens; the earliest printed accounts recommending their healing power also use those names.", "Here at Weissenbach, not far from the present Schongau baths, lived a rich man who had become leprous through his excesses. He had tried every physician and remedy in vain. Shunning humanity and himself avoided, he now crept through the great forest that once covered this entire region, of which only several mighty oaks remain today. The evil spirit suggested that he could cleanse himself and be freed from the disease by bathing in the blood of seven or twelve virtuous maidens, and all his striving now turned toward obtaining such blood. On the highest ridge, where a tall oak rose far above the forest, he succeeded in attacking a group of eleven girls who had taken the shaded path on their way to church from Boswil to Hitzkirch. He caught them and hanged them all together from the oak’s low-hanging branches. What good did it do him? The twelfth was still missing, and he remained unclean. On Monday, he immediately rode down to the mill and sang such sweet and flattering songs up toward the miller’s daughter’s window that the inexperienced child allowed herself to be enticed onto his horse. He instantly galloped with his captive toward the terrible place in the forest. When the girl recognized her fate, she pleaded, ‘Let me make only three more calls before I must die.’ He granted her request and said:", "‘Here we are between forest and field;\nNeither God nor the world can hear you.\nScream as loudly as you can.’", "She now called the names of her father, mother, and brother. The murderer laughed maliciously because he knew that her father sat at a wedding feast in the Star Inn and that her brother was hunting far away. He did not fear the old mother. But when she heard her daughter’s call and felt herself too weak, she allowed the voice to blow across into the forests of Boswil. There, the hunting brother stopped and listened:", "‘Hush! Do I hear my little hunting dogs,\nOr the voice of our little child?’", "He immediately rode after the voice and burst upon the place where the villain was about to hang his sister from the oak with a willow band. He freed the girl, bound the murderer with the same restraint to the tail of his black horse, and dragged him to death through the fir forest. Thus the man of blood was executed. At that time, the healing spring arose beside the murder tree. People also say that the two who escaped vowed to establish baths there, but first had to dig for the water and then have it heated. The shade of the executed Bluebeard still walks upon the height whenever the weather is about to change.
