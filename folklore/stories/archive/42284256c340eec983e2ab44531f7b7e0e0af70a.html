He could no longer get out, for the walls were smooth and overhanging. After struggling and trying in vain, he resigned himself, for better or worse, to his fate and began crawling around his prison. Before long he entered a long passage whose walls dripped with moisture. His eyes grew accustomed to the darkness and soon perceived a dreadful dragon that clung to the wall with its feet and licked up the moisture. At first the hunter was terrified, but the animal did him no harm. It merely looked at him and then gestured that he too should lick the rock. Since he truly felt hunger and thirst, he followed the signal and licked with his tongue the liquid slowly seeping down. The hunter became accustomed to his food and to the creature, his only companion possessing life and something like fellow feeling. Many years may have passed in this way when, one day, the dragon let itself slide down from the wall, crawled out through the passage, thrust its head out of the Krachen, and placed first its forelegs and then its hind legs on the ground. It left its tail hanging down into the cave for quite some time and made all sorts of movements to indicate that the hunter should cling to it. But the man did not understand the dragon’s language, so the creature flew away alone. A terrible sense of emptiness and abandonment now crept over the poor lonely man in his cave. He felt as though his only friend had been taken from him; tears even came to his eyes. “And you see, after all, it was only an animal, and not even a beautiful one!” To his greatest joy, after some time the one he missed so sorely came rushing back and resumed their old life. The hunter would have liked to throw his arms around its neck. When, after a long, long time, the dragon again prepared to fly out, it finally occurred to the prisoner to sit upon its tail and hold fast with his hands. The creature rose slowly and carefully set its burden down upon the ground. The freed hunter hurried home. But how everything had changed! He did not know a single one of the people he met. A different house stood at home, and none of its occupants wanted anything to do with him; he himself knew no one either. In vain he gave his name and claimed to be the owner of the house. At last they went to the priest. In the old books, he found that a chamois hunter of that name had disappeared 105 years before. Thus the hunter had spent 105 years in the cave in the dragon’s company! But now his life quickly approached its end. He could no longer tolerate food; everything he consumed had to come back up, and he felt as though a lump of lead lay in his stomach.
