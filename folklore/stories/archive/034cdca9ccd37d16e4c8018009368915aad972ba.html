High on the slopes above the little village of Bütz, not far from an isolated farmhouse, lies a wild little spring. People passing by on summer evenings have seen a young woman sitting here with a sorrowful expression, braiding the hair that flows down over her neck and shoulders. During the 1820s, the priest of Gansingen rode through this place. As he approached the little spring, the sacristan accompanying him asked him to dismount carefully, because the horse might easily shy at the apparition of the woman. But the priest said that, in the service of the Church, he had no business with spirits, and he remained in the saddle. Nevertheless, the steed reared at the spring and threw its rider, though he suffered no real harm. From that time on, whenever the priest had to cross the mountain, he carefully dismounted near the spring and led the horse past by the reins.
