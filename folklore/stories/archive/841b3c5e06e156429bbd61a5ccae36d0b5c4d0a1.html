At the crossroads by the village, he saw frogs restlessly hopping over one another in such a narrow space that they seemed to have been trained to do it. So great a number of frogs within so small an area did seem strange to him, but at the same moment he remembered the Basel gift that he had once again promised and once again forgotten. Today, moreover, it had grown much too late. He knew that his wife did not make empty threats. It therefore seemed a very good idea to put half a dozen of these frogs in a sack and give them to his wife at home in place of the market trinket. Upstairs, in the light, she tried to open the tightly tied sack while her husband busied himself below with the wagon so as to avoid the commotion. Half a dozen weighty gold coins rolled onto the table, shining as though they had come sparkling new from the mint. While the woman examined the coins, the carter entered the room and could scarcely trust either his wife or, still less, himself when he saw and learned into what Goldfüchse the ugly frogs had changed.
