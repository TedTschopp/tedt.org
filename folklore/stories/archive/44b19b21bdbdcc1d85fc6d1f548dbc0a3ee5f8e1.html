At the scene of the crime, he buried the body and then, tormented by his conscience, fled into foreign military service. Once a goatherd noticed that one of his goats, grazing by that Geissbergerstein, had scratched several bones out of the undergrowth. Looking more closely, the boy found more and reported them to the priest. From various circumstances, people had some idea who had died there, but no one guessed the murderer. The bones were then buried in the consecrated ground of the cemetery. After seven years, Chnoche Dönel returned home from the war and learned that his beloved had died and was to be buried that day. Grief drove him to the churchyard, where the gravedigger was preparing her resting place. At that very moment, the gravedigger lifted a dazzling white little bone from the grave in which poor Brosi’s earthly remains had been laid and showed it in wonder to the bystanders. One person took it in hand, examined it, and then passed it to another, until it also came to Dönel. Concealing his pounding heart, he boldly took the little bone in his hand, only to become rigid with fear and deathly pale, for the bone bled in his grasp. Everyone knew at once what this meant. Dönel was arrested as the murderer and executed.
