In some places these processions of the dead are also called Volkgang or Symphonie. Anyone who strays into such a humming and murmuring procession of spirits, or lets one overtake him, falls prey to a serious illness and must often suffer from it for weeks and months. The people also believe they know these passages and paths. He thought he recognized the voice of his dead father, quickly answered that he would go up as soon as possible and remove the obstruction, dressed himself, strode up the slope with long steps, and set to work. Just as he was dragging the last tree from the path, he heard the same voice again: “Hurry, hurry, and stand on the right side of the path!”", "With all his strength he pulled at the final round log and, exhausted almost to death, sat down beside the path upon one of the trunks. Among these sounds he heard all kinds of music echoing in the rocks, then weeping and laughing voices, a confused rushing and whispering. A warm breath of wind blew upon him, and suddenly a gust passed through the timber and made his hair stand on end. Strain his eyes as he might, he could distinguish nothing but black shadows flitting hurriedly past. When the clock in the church tower below struck twelve, he perceived figures passing two and four abreast, as the width of the path allowed. Some were well dressed, some walked barefoot, and still others laboriously dragged two coats. A woman bore a heavy Butterballe upon her head in place of a hat.
