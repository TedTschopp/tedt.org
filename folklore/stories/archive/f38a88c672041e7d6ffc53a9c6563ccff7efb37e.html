In still earlier times, the settlement of Belfort stood there and was likewise buried. Plurs was a rich little town, and where much money and gold lie, Satan is accustomed to settle and cast his snares. Thus it happened that the inhabitants of Plurs gave themselves over to luxury and gluttony. Pride also entered among them, and vanity and wickedness filled the measure. It was on a beautiful summer day, August 24 in the year 1618, when a wedding was celebrated in Plurs. All the guests appeared in velvet and silk, the bridal couple were hung with gold chains and precious stones, and the road to the church was laid with velvet carpets over which they walked before the altar. After the marriage, people ate from silver plates and drank from golden goblets. Cinnamon bark burned beneath the pots in the kitchen, and the hall walls glittered entirely with precious objects. After the meal, the wedding company strolled along the Maira. On the bank, a snow-white lamb skipped around its mother. Someone in the company conceived the idea of skinning the lamb’s silver-curled fleece for the spectators’ amusement. No sooner thought than done—in a few minutes the bleating lamb stood with its flesh bare, staggered a few steps forward, then fell dead. Not one member of the company tried to prevent the wicked deed. They returned to the little town and danced into the night. The dance had not yet ended when, amid terrible thunder, the neighboring mountain buried the town and every living soul within it." ], "notes": [] } } ---
