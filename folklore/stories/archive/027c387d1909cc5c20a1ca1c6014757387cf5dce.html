When Lerjen told him that he had seen a beautiful, snow-white chamois in the fearsome Gredetsch mountains but had been unable to get at it, the priest urged him not to go hunting again in the future. Once, when the priest had to carry out verwahren for a gravely ill person in Brigisch during the night, he met Big Lerjen on his return journey, while it was still dark before dawn. With his rifle on his shoulder and fully equipped for the high-mountain hunt, dripping with sweat, Lerjen came toward him in such haste that he seemed greatly pressed. ‘Where, where are you going in such a rush, Lerjen?’ the priest asked him. ‘To Gredetsch, to get the white animal—whatever it costs!’ he replied. ‘Surely you cannot be serious?’ the priest asked again. ‘Serious, serious!’ Lerjen replied. ‘Well then, farewell; we shall not see each other again!’ said the priest. He warmly shook Lerjen’s hand once more—and continued on his way. That evening the shepherds brought news that a hunter had fallen to his death in the Gredetsch mountains. The white animal was said to have lured him onto the most dangerous rocky paths, from which he plunged into the dizzying abyss. The unfortunate Lerjen was found shattered into small pieces; his remains were bundled together in a linen cloth and brought to the churchyard in Naters." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “verwahren”: although the ordinary German verb can mean ‘to keep safe,’ in this Valais Catholic context it denotes a priest attending a gravely ill person with the church’s final sacraments, closest to administering the last rites; the passage does not identify the precise sacrament or rites performed." ] } } ---
