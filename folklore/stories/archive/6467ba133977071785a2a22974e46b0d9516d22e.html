They left her sitting in her hermitage outside in the Jura, behind rock and forest, beside her cold spring, and did not listen to her. But once, during a famine, she had forty sacks of flour placed around her cell and baked Krapfen. Soon the way was no longer too far for the city children. They came running in great numbers and wanted to eat rolls. She gave one to each child and recited the creed to each in turn. Her flour did not run out, and the bread grew between the children’s teeth; soon the first school for small children was complete. But the envious Devil wants his stone in everything. It did not suit him that the young should become different from their elders, so he tore a piece of rock from the Jura wall and hurled it down toward the cell. It came to rest just above the roof. The black block of stone can still be seen there today, with as many holes as the jealous Devil then pressed into it with his claws. The saint could no longer remain here. She took a millstone that lay ready for loading beside the Aare at Solothurn and floated downstream on it into Aargau. As she passed the little town of Klingnau, all the bells inside began to ring of themselves. In those days the people of Klingnau had built their houses so that every window faced into the town and none looked out toward the river; they therefore could not see the saint pass and were even less able to explain the ringing, for which no special permission had been obtained. They ran to the town sexton and immediately gave him a proper Kavelantes—a severe scolding. They broke open the tower doors to punish the wicked boys whom they supposed had climbed inside, but were greatly astonished when they saw the bell ropes being drawn up and down by an invisible hand. Now they recognized a miracle, and from the tower they soon discovered the saint out in the current, just passing the broad headland of the island called Au. While they opened a grand council deliberation and put forward a series of proposals for the select honors with which delegations should greet Verena and escort her into the town, she had already landed far away in the desolate Giritz, an Aare lowland then occupied only by venomous marsh creatures. Here Verena raised three fingers to heaven and thrust them into the sand of the riverbank. At once the Verenabrünnlein sprang forth, whose ever-clear water is still considered healing.
