Not far from the small settlement, the path leads through a wilderness of great boulders overgrown with mosses, bramble bushes, and other climbing plants. Anyone who examines the stone wilderness more closely notices that the masses fell from the rock wall above the sloping scree, but no one suspects that the old village of Täsch lies buried beneath this rubble. In holy seasons, Quatemberkinder hear the little bell of the shattered church; it sounds exactly like the hum of a Brummel flying over star-like blossoms.", "At the time when the little village still stood, a poorly dressed man with a handsome full blond beard approached the richest farmer’s wife, who was just boiling potatoes in a great kettle. The man’s shirt was open over his chest, and a black slouch hat shaded his gaunt face. He begged the farmer’s wife to give him alms from her abundance. He had been turned away at every house, had eaten nothing all day, and felt the greatest hunger. The farmer’s wife made an angry face, thrust out her arm to ward him off, and shook her right hand. She was not accustomed to inviting beggars and idlers to her table; he could work and earn his own bread. With that, she slammed the door. The beggar straightened up and said: “Oh, you foolish woman! Had you granted me alms, such a blessing would have entered your food that you would always have had enough to eat. Since you are so miserly and hard-hearted, you and your village shall be cursed and damned!” With that he went away and directed his steps toward the village’s last hut. It was a wooden house blackened with age, with two blind little windows. The rich woman watched him through the window and laughed: “Let him knock there; the widow has nothing to bite into herself!” The poor widow, however, did not turn the stranger away but said: “I am poor and cannot offer you much, but if you are willing to wait a little, I shall fetch my chicken, which is outside on the manure heap searching for scattered grain chaff, and slaughter it!” The stranger agreed, took a seat at the humble little table, and the woman cooked the chicken and set it before him. The man thanked her warmly and wished to continue his journey, but the woman asked him to remain there overnight, for a storm was approaching and the next village was far away. If he was willing to make do with her bed, he should have it; she would find some little place for herself. The stranger thanked her again and said that he would gladly accept the bed, since he had traveled a long way that day and was very tired. Before lying down to rest, he told the woman that during the night she would hear a dreadful din, rumbling, and crashing, and that the house would tremble as in an earthquake, but she should not be afraid and should not light a lamp.", "And so it happened: during the night she heard a thunderous crashing as though the world must burst apart. The mountain above the village split, fell down, and buried the village together with the church and all its huts. In the morning no trace of it could be seen, not a beam or a roof board; house-sized boulders lay in place of the huts and storehouses. Only the widow’s little house still stood, and she heard the chicken she had served her guest the evening before clucking on the manure heap.
