Merkur 1835, 121." ], "english_paragraphs": [ "On each of the three Friday nights before Good Friday, a poor woman of Laufenburg dreamed that at 12 noon on that day she could raise a considerable treasure at a certain place in the ruin there called Habsburg, the third ruin of that name in Switzerland. Popular belief maintains that whatever one dreams on such nights comes true; and because exactly the same dream had returned to her three times in succession, she resolved to act and went out at the appointed time to the lonely castle tower. At the first stroke of noon, a faint trembling shuddered through the ground beneath her feet, and very near her she saw a large pot beside which sat a black cat with fiery eyes that was just then moving away from it. The woman immediately approached and removed its lid, but instead of seeing it filled with gold, she saw only shards of glass. She went home in vexation and told a Capuchin friar what had happened. He reproached her severely and placed all the blame upon her doubtfulness alone for the treasure's undergoing so mocking a transformation.", "That black cat had been the castle spirit; in more recent times, it has given another sign of itself. A Jahrzeit was founded in the Laufenburg town church in memory of the lords of Laufenburg Castle, and every year a Mass for their souls is to be said for them. When this gradually fell into oblivion, violent snorting and groaning were heard at the ruin at night. Since then, the endowed Mass has once again been said as is proper.—Schweiz.
