The most beautiful Alpen are its dwelling places. Its favorite abode, however, is the Steinbergalp on the southeastern slope of the Hohgant, where a place bears its name. It loves human beings and protects them from the wild activity of evil spirits in the mountains. When these withdraw in spring with the melting snow, it passes with a warm wing over the grazing grounds and slopes and coaxes the first flowers from the rigid earth. It shows animals the best pasture places, where Bränderli and Alpengarbe grow. But it wishes to perform its good deeds in silence and grows angry when people speak of it. The people of the Oberland therefore do not readily tell of the Hauri, for they need its aid. In winter, it protects people from the destructive enemies of the air. Thus, when goblins have scraped together an avalanche to hurl down upon human dwellings, a lamenting voice is heard in the air warning those in danger. Often, the voice calls a person's name; often, it is only a peculiar whimpering sound from the place where the danger threatens. It warns twice. The third time, however, it is as though earth and sky were crying woe. A howling lament breaks from every chasm in the mountains, all the air groans in anxious sorrow, and the Hauri darts over the threatened place like a flash of storm light. Immediately upon its heels follows the horror of destruction: whole mountains of snow roll dully and thunderously from the heights, while the mountain spirits, laughing scornfully and riding upon torn-loose fragments of rock that they spur into wild leaps, plunge down upon the place of devastation. A short time before an avalanche buried the Grimselspital high in the Oberhasli mountains several years ago, the servant who guarded the house alone through winter heard the Hauri. It called lamentingly from the Juchliberg. The dogs sprang up uneasily, opened the doors themselves, and fled outside. Believing that a traveler was calling for help, the servant also hurried before the door. Outside, the sun shone brightly and pleasantly. But something hovered in the air around the Juchliberg; he could not see clearly what it was. No traveler appeared near or far. He called the dogs, which wandered aimlessly about, and returned with them to his work in the room. Then the sound rang out a second time. Again, he sought the supposed traveler who had called for his aid, and again in vain. But a reddish glow shimmered at the Juchli. He returned to the house. Only when the sky above him seemed to collapse with a terrible roar did he recognize the warning cry—but too late. The sturdy roof beams were snapped like straws, and immeasurable burdens of snow had rolled before the doors. Every exit appeared blocked. Only the strength of the walls had preserved the house from complete destruction and him from a dreadful death. But in its flight, the Hauri had thrown open the cover of the chimney, thereby opening an escape route for him. Carefully, he climbed through the chimney into daylight and carried news of the terrible devastation into the valley." ], "notes": [ "Swiss term “Hauri”: the local name of a benevolent Oberland mountain spirit that protects people and warns of avalanches; the story does not securely derive the name.", "Swiss term “Alpen”: here the plural of Alp, the high-mountain summer pastures and their working landscapes rather than the Alps as a mountain chain.", "Swiss term “Steinbergalp”: the named Alp on the Hohgant where the Hauri especially dwells; a particular place there also bears the spirit's name.", "Swiss term “Bränderli”: a local alpine plant-name marking good pasture selected by the Hauri; the source does not securely identify the botanical species.", "Swiss term “Alpengarbe”: an alpine plant-name, literally an ‘Alp sheaf’ or yarrow-like herb; the exact species intended by the historical source is uncertain.", "Swiss term “Grimselspital”: the historic mountain hospice at the Grimsel Pass; Spital here means a refuge or hospice for travelers, not only a modern hospital." ] } } ---
