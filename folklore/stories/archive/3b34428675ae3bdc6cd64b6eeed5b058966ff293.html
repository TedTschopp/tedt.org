In the knights’ hall, a graceful maiden appeared to him and begged him to save her, for she was condemned to wander through the castle’s rooms every night in the form of a serpent. If someone had the courage to kiss her three times over the course of three nights, he would free her from the enchantment and receive her hand and treasures as his reward.", "With a joyful heart, the young warrior agreed and was shown to a chamber where he was to spend the night. There he found every possible comfort and, on the table, the most delicious foods and very finest wines. After enjoying the meal, he lay down and slept. At precisely twelve o’clock, however, the sleeper was awakened by a hideous serpent that reared hissing beside his bed. The knight overcame his revulsion at the dreadful animal and kissed it upon the jaws. At once, a wonderfully beautiful girl’s head replaced the ugly serpent head; then the entire apparition vanished, and the knight slept peacefully on. The haunting was repeated the following night. The knight bravely embraced the serpent’s body and pressed a kiss upon it, and the monster became a charming maiden whose body, however, ended in a scaly tail. On the third night, the serpent maiden appeared again and, after the knight’s final kiss, assumed a completely human form. Early in the morning, the rescued maiden approached the knight as he strolled in the garden and asked him to meet her in three days before the church in the nearby village. She would then place her hand in his forever. The knight did as the maiden had told him and descended from the castle to a lonely inn in the middle of the forest. When he prepared to braid his queue the next morning, the innkeeper came into the room and asked to assist him with the task. The young man accepted her service, and with dainty hands the innkeeper braided the queue, but she inserted the needle of forgetfulness into the plaited hair. He forgot his bride and lived on thoughtlessly in splendor and delight. On the third day, the freed maiden waited for him. When he did not come, she sent her faithful old maid to him, and he promised to come the next morning. But the wicked innkeeper, who was an evil sorceress, again offered her services and repeated her magic, so that the nobleman forgot the time. After the sixth day, the maid returned and reported, with tears in her eyes, that the evil sorcerer had regained power over her mistress and that she now dwelt on the Glass Mountain. Only the knight could free her, because, as a child born on Sunday, he had power over evil spirits. The terrible news awakened the young man from his half-conscious dream, and he swore to hurry away and rescue his bride. The old maid gave him a pair of golden shoes with which he traveled three miles at every step, and so he reached the Glass Mountain while it was still broad daylight. Countless maidens with the most beautiful eyes begged him to save them, but he did not allow himself to be beguiled and did not rest until he found his bride, who embraced him jubilantly. Because he had only one pair of three-mile shoes, he gave one of them to his rescued beloved, put his arms around her, and sped with his precious burden as fast as lightning to the distant, now disenchanted castle. There the two became a happy couple and the ancestors of a great and powerful family." ], "notes": [] } } ---
