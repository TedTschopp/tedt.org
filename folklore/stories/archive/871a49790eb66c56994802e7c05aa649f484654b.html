For a long time, they secretly pursued their sweet affair. Then one evening, the Nixe appeared before her beloved with tearful eyes and said, “This is the last time you will see me. My father, who rules in the lake's depths, discovered my repeated absences and called me to account, and I confessed everything. He fell into a violent rage and forbade me to associate with you forever unless you follow me into the depths and live with me as my husband in wedlock.” “But how can that happen?” the youth replied with a sigh. “Water is not my element, and you are asking for my death.” “Not at all!” was the Wasserfräulein's comforting answer. “Drink this water, and beneath the flood you will be able to live as well as a fish.” The lover drank, faithfully dived below with the Nixe, and for a time lived magnificently and joyfully in the crystal palaces of the lake king beside his beautiful wife. Gradually, however, painful homesickness crept into his heart. He thought of his parents, siblings, and friends in the upper world; of the pleasures of his old home at weddings, wrestling matches, and the Alpfahrt; and of the joys of heaven to which bells and organ music called him. Deeply concerned for her ever-sadder husband, the Nixe finally coaxed the secret of his feelings from him and resolved to satisfy his longing as far as possible. One night, she replaced all the water in the kitchens of the two streets with the liquid that enables human nature to live beneath the waves. On the following morning, amid the joys of a festival day, those two streets suddenly sank into the lake. None of those who sank drowned. Their homes came to rest undamaged upon the lake bed, and the town clerk's son was reunited with his parents, relatives, acquaintances, and friends. Beneath a very clear sky, especially sharp eyes can make out not only the gables of the houses but also the busy activity in the streets. The bells of the sunken church often sound from the depths, mingled with wondrous organ music.
