The life of this noble old man ended beneath the murderous blow of his own Schwäher, Jost von Rudenz. Pursued by the dying man's curse, the murderer fled into the high mountains of the Oberland. In their ravines he thought himself safe from pursuit. There he built a hut for himself at the edge of a cliff. One deep night there came a knock at his hut door. When he opened it, outside stood a stranger wrapped in a long cloak who asked for shelter. Scarcely had Jost stepped before the hut when the nocturnal wanderer drew a long sword from beneath his cloak and swung it terribly through the air. In horror, Jost saw by moonlight words written in blood upon the sharp blade: ‘In the Devil's name, parricide, die!’—‘Go to Hell, monster!’ shouted the wanderer, and with the terrible weapon he pinned the murderer to the wall of the hut. Since then, the unfortunate man's soul must wander unredeemed through the mountains. His spirit still bears the sword in its heart. Whenever disaster threatens somewhere in the land, Jost's lost soul lets out a dreadful howl that echoes far through the mountains. Each time the heart-rending cry sounds three times.
