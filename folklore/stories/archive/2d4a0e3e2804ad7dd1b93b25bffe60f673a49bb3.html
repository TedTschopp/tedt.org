A young journeyman, tired from traveling, took lodging at an inn and remained there several days without considering that his depleted purse would not withstand it. But there was one person less careless than he who reckoned up the bill one evening: the innkeeper. Tomorrow, it was said, payment must be made. Thus came a night full of anxious cares for the reveler. In his distress, a black figure stepped to his bed and straightforwardly revealed himself as — the Devil. Yet he had not come to harm him, he said, but to help, provided the journeyman would likewise help him to a fortunate outcome. Neither the young man’s life nor his soul would be at stake. On the contrary, he would fare very well and, after a certain time had elapsed, fare still better. Indeed, the Devil would not even approach his deathbed. What the Devil demanded was only this: the man was to remain in this inn for seven years and, during that period, never wash or comb himself or cut his hair and nails. In return, he was to have food and drink in abundance and always money as plentiful as leaves. After the seven years, he would be as completely free as he had been before, would have nothing whatever to fear from the Devil, who, as stated, would not even approach his deathbed, and would instead always continue to receive enough money. What will recklessness not do? The journeyman had no hesitation about entering into the pact with the Evil One, although he did not know what the Devil was truly planning. At last five, then six years had passed, and the man looked dreadful. He fared well, that is true, very well. But in outward appearance he was as hideous as sin.", "One day a neighbor, a merchant, came to the innkeeper. He did a poor job of concealing the gnawing care in his heart beneath his merchant’s expression. As a friend, the innkeeper showed concern, and the other disclosed his suffering. As things will happen, great losses threatened him with ruin. Nowhere could he raise the missing sum. A rescuing thought flashed through the innkeeper’s mind. \"A peculiar fellow has lived with me for more than six years,\" he said. \"He is very good-natured, obliging, and generous, denies himself nothing, but looks like the ‘God preserve us from it.’ How could it be otherwise when a man always carries upon himself whatever sticks to him and lets whatever grows continue to grow? He is probably not indifferent to your daughters either, for I have often noticed that his eyes are not displeased to turn toward your house, and act as though they wished to spy something there.\" A person can resolve to do many things when in need. The merchant and the journeyman did indeed reach an agreement. The latter would furnish enough money if he were allowed to marry one of the three daughters. At home, the father presented his proposal to them. Admittedly, the two elder girls had already rejected many matches, for sometimes the young gentleman was not rich and witty enough, and sometimes not handsome and gallant enough. The father knew this well and brought the first and eldest to the strange suitor only with great anxiety, although the man’s money would have suited the beautiful girl. But when she saw him, she fled as though from the Evil One, crying: \"I would rather jump into the water than take him.\" — The second behaved no differently and jeered as she left: \"I would rather hang myself than marry him.\" Both also gave the third a horrifying description of his form. \"Now you, my dear child,\" the father said to the youngest, \"are my very last hope. She herself kept her eyes lowered to the ground at all times and did not look at him at all, but once again resolutely promised to marry him. The wedding celebration was set, and the necessary money was paid. Meanwhile, the seven years of the pact came to an end. One day, a handsome and elegant young gentleman arrived before the merchant’s house in a splendid carriage glittering with gold and precious stones and revealed himself to the astonished inhabitants as — the youngest daughter’s bridegroom. What rejoicing this brought her! She had indeed expected her suitor now, but the noble awareness that she was making a generous sacrifice of filial love had not been able to drive every cloud of sorrow from her face. Now they fled like mist in the May sunshine. At last the wedding party went to church and then came out again in a stately procession. Only the two elder sisters had remained home in anger and — had both killed themselves, one on a nail and the other in the water. As the bridegroom stepped out through the church portal, he saw upon a roof before him, for the first time in seven years, the Devil.
