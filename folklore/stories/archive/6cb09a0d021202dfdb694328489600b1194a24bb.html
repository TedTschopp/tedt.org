One fine winter morning, a column of sturdy men from Intschi moved toward the Rosstock in the gloomy Leutschachalp above Amsteg, climbing slowly but steadily in single file to gather stacked wild hay. Between Chäserli and Heitersbiel, a headless man whom they did not know came toward them. He walked past the first man in the group, whose name was Inderkum, and immediately disappeared, so that when Inderkum looked back curiously, he could no longer see him. The stranger did not meet the others at all, for they had fallen somewhat behind. In the following summer, in the year 1860, Inderkum met with a fatal accident in that region while leading two Neesli onto the Rosstock. Peter Gamma of the Geissplatte at Geschenen, an old man more than eighty years of age, relates the following remarkable event as something he experienced himself:", "I was still a schoolboy when I was walking toward Abfrutt one day. A little below the large cross there, I met my Chrisägetti (confirmation godfather), but to my great astonishment I could not see his head. After I had passed him in silence and gone a few steps farther, I looked back at him once more. It now appeared as though he had pulled his Tschoopen over his head, and I thought to myself, “He wanted to play a joke and give you a fright.” That evening, I confronted him with the whole matter, but he denied it flatly and called me a fool. One month later, my godfather leaped across a narrow place in the Gescheneralp-Reuss near Abfrutt, where we boys had very often jumped across in jest and play. He slipped, however, on the rock slab that partly arches over the wild stream and fell into the raging water.
