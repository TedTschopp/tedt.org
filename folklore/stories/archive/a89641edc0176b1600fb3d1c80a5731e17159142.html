Late one autumn, he roamed the heights of the Tschingelfeldalp in pursuit of chamois. Because fortune did not favor him on the first day, he remained above. Late in the evening, he entered the abandoned Sennhütte on the Tschingelfeldboden, ate his supper, and climbed into the Gasteren to seek his night's rest. Before he fell asleep, he heard from far away the bells of a herd that seemed to be approaching the hut. Before it arrived, he jumped down, fetched his gun from the bench, and laid it beside him. Scarcely had he returned when strange herders drove the cattle into the stable. From their conversation, he soon realized they were father and son. They immediately made a fire and arranged the dairy implements in the hut, then milked the cows and began making cheese. When the whey had boiled, the son said, ‘Father, we ought to give milk to the hunter above as well.’ The father replied, ‘Then call him.’ The son immediately climbed up and said, ‘Hunter, come and refresh yourself with fresh milk.’ Without a word, and with a heavy heart, the hunter descended into the hut and sat at the rear upon the Turnerbank, where the son silently handed him a Gohn full of milk and a spoon. When the hunter was about to drink the milk, the son thrust a knife into the wall beside the huntsman's left side. The thrust caused him such severe pain in that side that, without a word, he set the milk upon the bench and returned to his bed. After the Sennen had finished making cheese, they gathered all the equipment they had brought and departed again with the livestock and everything else by the same way they had come. The hunter heard them for a long time in the far distance. He could no longer fall asleep. He rose, took his Stutzer, and set out for home. He continued to feel severe pain in his left side for a long time.
