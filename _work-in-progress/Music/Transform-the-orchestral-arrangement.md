**The Theme Formula**
> **A section:** static tonic pedal + simple/suspended harmony  
> **B section:** release the pedal + introduce adventurous harmony  
> **Glue:** common tones + economical voice leading + repeated rhythmic identity  
> **Resolution:** pivot or cadence back home

**Rules**
1. **Divide the theme into two contrasting halves.**  
   The first half establishes stability; the second creates development and surprise. This balance of simple and complex is the central formula.

2. **Anchor the first half with a low tonic pedal.**  
   Hold the tonic beneath the harmony. This grounds the music and makes everything above it feel nearly static.

3. **Keep the opening harmonically restrained.**  
   Use sparse changes, suspended chords, inversions, added ninths, or major sevenths. The melody can remain harmonically ambiguous or “non-committal.”

4. **Release the pedal in the second half.**  
   Removing the pedal makes the harmonic motion feel faster, even if the actual rate of chord changes remains unchanged.

5. **Move from expected harmony to one conspicuous surprise.**  
   Establish a comprehensible progression, move briefly outside the key, grab the listener’s attention, and then return home.

6. **Reinterpret melody notes as pivots.**  
   A melody note can serve as the root, third, fifth, seventh, or extension of many chords. Hold the note while changing its harmonic meaning to enter a new tonal area.

7. **Choose development devices according to the desired character.**  
   Options demonstrated include borrowed chords, modal interchange, chromatic planing, direct modulation, secondary dominants, flat-II/Phrygian movement, and chromatic passing chords.

8. **Prioritize voice leading over theoretical labels.**  
   Retain common tones, use inversions, and move the remaining voices by small intervals. The presenters repeatedly identify this economical movement as more important than how the progression is named.

9. **Preserve a recognizable rhythmic cell.**  
   Repeating the same rhythm allows the harmony to travel further without the theme losing its identity.

10. **Return through a clear harmonic mechanism.**  
    Use a common-tone pivot, dominant cadence, or secondary dominant to make the return to the tonic feel earned.

**Practical Eight-Bar Template**

| Bars | Function | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Establish | Low tonic pedal, short motif, restrained suspended or added-note harmony |
| 5–6 | Depart | Remove pedal, preserve a common tone, introduce borrowed or unexpected chords |
| 7 | Intensify | Continue through smooth inversions, chromatic movement, or modulation |
| 8 | Resolve | Pivot or dominant cadence back to the tonic while repeating the core rhythmic cell |

The deeper rule is straightforward: **complexity works because stability came first.**
