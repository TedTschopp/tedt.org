Small Models, Useful Questions

Game theory studies choices whose outcomes depend on other people’s choices. Here, you can inspect the points each player earns, replay the same random sequence, and ask which assumptions fit your own situation.

Every experiment includes step-by-step math using pre-algebra, a separate expert section with formal equations, and a scenario link. The numbers describe the model. The questions are yours to take into a team, a negotiation, or a shared project.

The learning-by-playing approach is inspired by Nicky Case’s The Evolution of Trust. These experiments use their own models and implementation.

Choose Your Own Path

Start with the first six experiments if game theory is new to you. Then follow a question that interests you: sharing a lake, negotiating a split, choosing a voting rule, or rewarding good work.

Each experiment stands on its own. The final Mechanism Design Studio brings the ideas together: choose an allocation rule, then test what it encourages people to report.

These are small teaching models. Their assumptions and tie-breaking rules are part of the explanation, so you can see what a result does and does not establish.
