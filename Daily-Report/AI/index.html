Claude Goes Mobile — and Keeps Working While You Sleep

Anthropic announced that Claude Cowork is expanding to web and mobile, enabling AI agents to continue executing tasks remotely across devices even when a user's computer is offline. Persistent sessions, scheduled tasks, and cloud-based execution are now part of the package.

Why it matters: This is a meaningful shift from AI as a desktop productivity layer to AI as a persistent enterprise worker. For organizations already running Claude in knowledge-work, coding, or research workflows, the implications are operational: agents can now run overnight, across time zones, and on mobile approval flows without requiring a connected endpoint. The security and data governance questions this raises — particularly around persistent session credentials, data residency, and access scope — are not trivial and should not be deferred.

What should I do as a leader? Establish a clear policy for AI persistent-session use before broad adoption. Define what data agents may access during unattended execution, and ensure your identity and access management controls extend to agent sessions.

What should I do? If you are using Claude for multi-step research or drafting workflows, evaluate whether persistent mobile sessions would close gaps in your current process — and review what permissions any agent would hold in your name.

Meta's Muse Models Bring Agentic Media Generation to Scale

Meta introduced Muse Image and previewed Muse Video — next-generation generative AI media models featuring agentic image generation, self-refinement, multi-reference editing, and direct integration across Instagram, WhatsApp, and Meta AI. The models are designed to act on creative intent iteratively, not just respond to a single prompt.

Why it matters: For enterprises in marketing, media, advertising, or creative production, agentic media generation at platform scale changes cost and speed assumptions significantly. More immediately, Meta's integration of these capabilities directly into consumer platforms means the volume of AI-generated visual content reaching your customers, employees, and markets is about to increase sharply. Authenticity standards, content provenance policies, and vendor use terms all need review.

What should I do as a leader? Update your organization's AI content policy to address AI-generated media provenance — especially for regulated industries. Evaluate Muse-class capabilities as part of any current creative workflow RFP.

What should I do? Start familiarizing yourself with content attribution and disclosure norms for AI-generated media in your professional context. What is acceptable in your industry or role may be shifting faster than internal guidelines reflect.
