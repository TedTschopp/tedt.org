Big Moves

Sierra's Pinecone Turns Workforce Expertise Into Reusable AI

Sierra, the enterprise AI platform company, published details on Pinecone — an internal AI agent designed to capture how employees actually work: their workflows, discoveries, and hard-won best practices. Rather than letting that knowledge evaporate when someone moves teams or leaves the company, Pinecone packages it into reusable AI-powered capabilities that any employee or agent can draw from.

The framing is simple and the ambition is significant. Most enterprises spend years building knowledge management systems that go stale the moment they're complete. Pinecone's approach — capturing knowledge as it flows through real work rather than asking employees to document it manually — addresses a problem that no intranet, wiki, or SharePoint deployment has ever fully solved.

Why it matters: Organizational knowledge loss is one of the least-quantified risks in enterprise AI planning. As workforce transitions accelerate and agentic AI takes on more routine tasks, the gap between what experienced employees know and what AI systems can execute widens. A system that actively narrows that gap — and builds trust while doing it — is a meaningful operational lever.

  • What should I do as a leader? Assess how your organization currently captures and distributes tacit knowledge. If that answer involves a wiki no one updates, look at how agentic workflows could automate the capture layer rather than the documentation layer.
  • What should I do? Pay attention to how your day-to-day AI tools learn from your work patterns. If they don't, ask your IT or AI team whether a capability like this is on the roadmap.

Model Routing Is an Enterprise Cost and Performance Lever — and It's Harder Than It Looks

IBM Research and Hugging Face published a detailed analysis of what production AI model routing actually requires. The core argument: sending hard queries to bigger models and easy ones to cheaper models sounds logical in theory, but optimizing across cost, latency, quality, and reliability simultaneously creates tradeoffs that don't resolve cleanly — and that shift as your workloads evolve.

The piece makes a point that deserves board-level attention in 2026: enterprises that deploy AI at scale will increasingly compete on how well they orchestrate across models, not just on which models they choose. Model routing is becoming an operational discipline, not a configuration setting.

Why it matters: Most enterprise AI deployments today run a single model per use case. As models multiply and cost pressure grows, the organizations that build routing competency early will have a structural efficiency advantage. The investment is modest; the operational return compounds over time.

  • What should I do as a leader? If your AI deployments currently use a single model for all requests in a category, commission a routing analysis. Even a simple two-tier approach — a capable frontier model for complex requests, a smaller fast model for routine ones — can materially reduce costs.
  • What should I do? If you help evaluate or procure AI tools, start asking vendors how their platforms handle model selection across requests, not just which single model powers the product.

Vendors, Platforms & Models

Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Releases Inkling, an Open-Weights Enterprise Model

Thinking Machines — founded by former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati — introduced Inkling, a multimodal open-weights mixture-of-experts model built for enterprise customization. It ships alongside Tinker, a fine-tuning platform designed to let organizations adapt the model to their specific data, workflows, and governance requirements without relying on a hosted API.

Inkling doesn't yet match the top frontier models on benchmarks, and the team acknowledges that openly. What it offers instead is something different: controllability and data ownership for organizations that need to fine-tune on sensitive internal data, operate in regulated industries, or simply prefer not to send proprietary workflows to a third-party cloud.

Why it matters: The open-weights model market is maturing rapidly, and Murati's credibility — and OpenAI pedigree — gives Inkling more weight than a typical new entrant. Enterprises evaluating AI infrastructure in the next six months should put Inkling on the shortlist alongside Llama and Mistral variants, particularly for use cases where fine-tuning on proprietary data is a requirement.

  • What should I do as a leader? If your organization has deferred custom model development because frontier APIs seemed good enough, revisit that calculus. The fine-tuning tooling has matured significantly, and open-weights options now offer a credible path to both customization and cost control.

Moonshot AI Releases Kimi K3 With 1M-Token Context at Competitive Pricing

Moonshot AI, one of China's leading AI labs, released Kimi K3 — a large multimodal model with a one-million-token context window and enterprise-oriented pricing. The model is designed for large-scale document analysis, extended reasoning chains, and workloads that exceed standard context limits.

Performance is competitive across most benchmark categories, though the team notes limitations in mathematical reasoning and consistency at the edges of the context window. Pricing is positioned to undercut comparable offerings from U.S. providers on a per-token basis.

Why it matters: For enterprises with workloads that require processing very long documents — contracts, clinical records, financial filings, technical manuals — one-million-token context is a meaningful capability difference. Kimi K3 adds another credible option to that tier. Procurement and legal teams will want to review data residency and sovereignty terms before deploying, given Moonshot's jurisdiction.

  • What should I do as a leader? If your organization processes long-form documents, evaluate whether your current models handle full-context analysis or require chunking workarounds. If the latter, the new generation of long-context models may simplify your architecture.

Security, Risk & Compliance

Perplexity Launches Secure Sandboxes for AI Agent Execution

Perplexity introduced Secure Sandboxes, an infrastructure layer that gives AI agents isolated execution environments, credential management, and stronger security boundaries when running autonomous tasks. The offering positions Perplexity as a platform for business-critical agentic workflows, not just search.

The category is crowded — similar sandboxing approaches exist from several vendors — but the direction is clear. As enterprises deploy agents that browse the web, run code, call APIs, and access internal systems, the question of where those operations execute and what credentials they can access becomes an urgent security architecture question.

Why it matters: Most enterprise security frameworks were built for humans interacting with systems, not AI agents acting autonomously. Sandboxed agent execution is the first step toward extending traditional security controls — least privilege, audit logging, credential isolation — to AI workflows. Organizations that build agent security architecture now will be ahead when regulators start asking the question formally.

  • What should I do as a leader? Ask your security team whether your current AI agent deployments have defined execution boundaries, credential scoping, and audit trails. If the answer is unclear, treat it as a gap to close before expanding agentic deployments.
  • What should I do? If you use AI tools that take actions on your behalf — scheduling, file management, API calls — ask your IT team what security controls are in place around those actions.
