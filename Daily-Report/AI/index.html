Vertical AI Is Quietly Winning at the Frontier

Harvey has published details on how it uses post-training, fine-tuning, synthetic environments, and open-weight models to build domain-specific AI that competes with — and in some legal-agent and M&A tasks, outperforms — general-purpose frontier models.

Why it matters: The next competitive layer in enterprise AI isn't a bigger foundation model; it's a better-trained vertical one. For legal, finance, and compliance teams, specialized models built on open-weight foundations may deliver stronger performance at lower cost. The question every enterprise AI team should ask: which workflows are high-stakes enough to warrant their own fine-tuning program?

What should I do as a leader? Map your highest-value workflows to the fine-tuning investment that makes vertical models viable. Start with one domain and define what "outperforms" looks like before you build.

Customer Support Agents at 100 Million Users: What Actually Works

A case study shows how customer-support AI agents operate at massive scale by combining LLM reasoning, tool use, information retrieval, and automated evaluation — including simulated customer conversations before deployment.

Why it matters: Rigorous evaluation science drove major gains in satisfaction and self-service resolution. Inter-rater reliability testing and production measurement mattered as much as model choice. For enterprises scaling support agents, this is a practical blueprint: define your evaluation methodology before you scale, not after.

What should I do as a leader? Before expanding agent coverage to new segments, implement automated eval pipelines that simulate real conversations. If your team can't measure when the agent is wrong today, that's the gap to close first.

Enterprise Ops & Adoption

Axonius Cuts Agent Development Time from Eight Weeks to Ten Days

Axonius used Amazon Bedrock AgentCore to build secure multi-tenant AI agents with VM-level sandbox isolation per tenant, reducing new-agent development time by roughly 75%.

Why it matters: Agent isolation is a non-negotiable requirement in multi-tenant environments — not optional security hardening. Enterprises planning production agent deployments should treat isolation patterns like these as a baseline before scaling.

What should I do as a leader? Include agent isolation requirements in your enterprise AI architecture review. If your current deployment doesn't define tenant boundaries at the infrastructure layer, address that before scaling.

Source: AWS Machine Learning Blog, 2026-08-21

Security, Risk & Compliance

Google's Mandiant Team Found Over 100 Vulnerabilities in Two Days Using AI Agents

Google's Mandiant team describes an agentic vulnerability discovery harness combining AI agents with deterministic controls to review source code — identifying more than 100 critical vulnerabilities in two days.

Why it matters: Automated, AI-driven code review can now move faster than conventional manual red-teaming cycles. The key design choice — pairing AI agents with deterministic controls rather than replacing controls — is the right model for enterprise adoption.

What should I do as a leader? Ask whether your current code-review toolchain is augmented by AI. If not, prioritize a structured evaluation of agentic vulnerability discovery tools in the next planning cycle.
