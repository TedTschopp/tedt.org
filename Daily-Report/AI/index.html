On July 2, Microsoft announced the creation of a new AI deployment unit backed by a $2.5 billion commitment. Amazon Mechanical Turk Closes to New Customers

Amazon announced July 2 that Mechanical Turk, the platform enabling businesses to distribute small digital tasks to human workers at scale, will stop accepting new customers. The Register reported the move as the quiet wind-down of a service that once powered significant portions of the AI training data and content moderation industry.

Why it matters: Mechanical Turk was a foundational data infrastructure layer for AI — it supplied the human-labeled training data that underpinned many of the models enterprises rely on today. Its closure signals that the economics of crowd-sourced human annotation have shifted; automated tools and AI-generated synthetic data have largely absorbed the workload. For organizations that still rely on MTurk-style annotation workflows — directly or through vendor contracts — now is the time to audit those dependencies and confirm continuity plans.

  • What should I do as a leader? Review your data labeling, annotation, and content moderation pipelines for exposure to Mechanical Turk or similar crowd-sourcing platforms. Identify vendor alternatives and evaluate whether AI-assisted labeling tools can meet your quality and volume requirements.
  • What should I do? If your team works with data vendors, ask specifically whether their workflows have any Mechanical Turk exposure and what their transition plan looks like.
