Big Moves

AT&T Is Quietly Shifting AI Spend to Open-Source Models

AT&T has moved roughly 40% of its enterprise AI workloads to open-source models — and is targeting 60 to 70% — significantly cutting its bills from proprietary providers like Anthropic and OpenAI. The driving forces are model routing, cost optimization, context engineering, and vendor portability.

Why it matters: AT&T's scale makes this a bellwether, not a niche experiment. If a global carrier with complex compliance requirements can route the majority of its AI traffic through open models, most enterprises can too. The trade-off is not free: managing model switching, harness-layer lock-in, and quality control across multiple providers requires organizational muscle. Cost savings are real, but so is the operational overhead of maintaining a multi-model fleet.

What should I do as a leader?

  • Conduct a model-spend audit: categorize workloads by required quality, sensitivity, and cost tolerance, then identify candidates for open-source substitution.
  • Build or buy a model router before committing further to any single proprietary vendor.

What should I do?

  • If you manage AI tools or prompts for your team, ask your platform team whether output quality has been validated on lower-cost model tiers for your specific use cases.

Zalando's Two-Year Agentic Infrastructure Snapshot

Zalando has published a candid look at the agentic engineering platform it built over 2.5 years to serve more than 200 product and engineering teams. The infrastructure includes shared gateways, authentication, observability, model discovery, and agent tooling — all designed so that the underlying LLM is interchangeable.

Why it matters: Zalando's core thesis — that competitive advantage now lives in the AI harness, not the model — is quickly becoming conventional wisdom among sophisticated enterprise AI teams. Shared context, memory, Model Context Protocol (MCP) infrastructure, access controls, and observability are the real differentiators. The cautionary note: Zalando's highly decentralized approach pushes risk management to individual teams, which creates meaningful AI governance exposure at scale.

What should I do as a leader?

  • Treat your AI harness (gateways, observability, access controls) as a strategic asset, not plumbing. Invest accordingly.
  • If your AI adoption is decentralized, establish a governance layer before it becomes a liability.

What should I do?

  • Ask whether your team's AI tools are connected to any shared observability or access-control layer — and if not, raise the question.

Anthropic Publishes Token-Cost Best Practices for Claude Code

Anthropic released detailed guidance on reducing Claude Code token consumption: keep sessions short, choose the right model tier, preserve prompt caches, and avoid changes that invalidate cached context.

Why it matters: As enterprises scale developer AI tools, token costs compound fast. Prompt caching is one of the highest-leverage levers available — but it only works if teams understand how context windows, model selection, and session management interact. The broader lesson: AI resource management is becoming a required competency for platform and engineering teams, not just a vendor-side concern.

What should I do as a leader?

  • Establish guidelines for AI developer tool usage — session length, model tier selection, and cache-friendly practices — before costs scale further.

What should I do?

  • If you use Claude Code or similar agentic coding tools, review Anthropic's best practices and adjust your workflow to preserve prompt cache across sessions.

Slack Code Brings Coding Agents Into Shared Team Channels

Slack has introduced Slack Code, which embeds coding agents directly into shared Slack channels so teams can collaborate around code, agent activity, and administrative controls inside their existing workflows.

Why it matters: Integrating agents into team communication channels reduces friction and makes AI activity visible to the whole team — a meaningful step toward making agentic software development a team sport rather than an individual power-user behavior. For enterprises already standardized on Slack, this is an incremental but practical extension. Governance teams should note that shared agent workspaces create new audit and access-control considerations.

What should I do as a leader?

  • Evaluate whether Slack Code fits your organization's developer workflow, and ensure your security team reviews agent permissions and channel access controls before broad rollout.
