AI Search Agents Fail at Asking, Not at Searching

A new AI evaluation framework called DiscoBench has produced a counterintuitive finding: AI search and research agents most often fail not because they search incorrectly, but because they don't pause to ask clarifying questions when a query is ambiguous. Models that search repeatedly on an unclear request perform significantly worse than agents configured to stop and ask a follow-up question before proceeding.

Why it matters: Enterprise deployments of AI research assistants, legal discovery tools, and knowledge management agents all depend on handling ambiguous inputs well. If your agents are configured to try harder rather than ask smarter, you may be receiving confident-sounding but misdirected outputs — which is more dangerous than an obvious failure. This benchmark result should directly inform how your teams evaluate and configure agentic AI tools.

What should I do as a leader? Ask your AI implementation team whether your deployed agents are configured to request clarification on ambiguous queries. If not, treat this as a priority quality-improvement item.

What should I do? When working with AI research or agent tools, write queries clearly and specifically. If the output seems off-target, explicitly ask the tool to confirm its interpretation before proceeding.

Your Job Postings May Be Too Long — and AI Is Why

Business Insider reports a notable and growing trend: job descriptions have expanded dramatically in length, many now resembling the notoriously verbose CVS pharmacy receipts. The driver appears to be AI writing tools used by HR teams and recruiters to draft or expand postings, resulting in bloated requirements lists and generic language that qualified candidates increasingly skip past or dismiss outright.

Why it matters: HR teams using generative AI to draft job postings may be creating a self-defeating cycle. Longer, AI-inflated postings tend to discourage qualified candidates who see unrealistic requirement stacks, while attracting applicants who treat the list as negotiable. The result is lower-quality candidate pipelines, not more complete job descriptions. In a competitive talent market, the cost of getting this wrong is real.

What should I do as a leader? Set a length and quality standard for job postings across your organization. If your talent acquisition team uses AI to generate postings, require a human editing pass that cuts rather than adds.

What should I do? If you write job descriptions or source candidates, audit your three most recent postings for AI-generated inflation — look for requirement lists that no real candidate is likely to fully meet, and trim them to what you actually need.
